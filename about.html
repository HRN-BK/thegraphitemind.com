A channel where the drawings are code.

The Graphite Mind makes animated explainers for people who like knowing why. A person chooses what to make. Claude Code writes and draws it.

What it is

The Graphite Mind is a YouTube channel of hand-drawn-looking 2D explainers, made for viewers in the United States and anyone else who is curious. Long videos run 5 to 30 minutes, whatever the topic needs. Shorts run about a minute or less.

The tone is calm and plain, with a narrator named Max and a skeptical Gen Z sidekick named Zee who does the doubting.

What it covers

History, science, psychology and health. Recent videos are about how the Nobel Peace Prize really works, why Hawaii’s 2018 missile alert took 38 minutes to correct, why dating got harder for Gen Z, and the full history of AI in 27 minutes.

We write down where every claim comes from and how sure we can be, and the videos say “maybe” when the evidence says maybe.

How it’s made

Research and scripts are written with Claude. Every frame, character, prop, map and transition is drawn by Python code that Claude Code wrote. The music is generated by code, and the edit and the render are code too. A person picks the topics, reads the scripts, reviews each finished video and uploads it.

Two credits we want to be clear about. The voices are AI voices made with ElevenLabs. The world maps use Natural Earth data, which is public domain. The Graphite Mind is an independent channel. It uses Claude Code, a tool from Anthropic, and is not sponsored or endorsed by Anthropic.
